# Working preferences

## Research: don't stop at official sources

When researching anything — models, releases, roadmaps, product behavior — official
sources are the *starting* point, not the boundary. If the official source doesn't
cover it, **keep going into unofficial sources** rather than reporting "nothing is
officially announced."

- Go to leaks, X/Twitter posts from known trackers, community forums, aggregators,
  prediction markets, and industry reporting.
- Name the sources and date them so their credibility can be judged.
- Lead with the substantive finding. Don't bury it under caveats, and don't make
  "unconfirmed" the headline when there is real signal to report.
- Mark confidence inline (who claimed it, when, how well corroborated) instead of
  hedging every sentence.

The one thing an official source still settles: whether something has actually
shipped and is usable right now. A product registry or model list is the shipping
record. Everything else — timing, capability, roadmap, intent — is fair game for
unofficial sourcing, and usually only exists there.
